FHA vs conventional loans

The two most common ways to buy a home, side by side. Which fits depends on your credit, down payment and plans.

FHA and conventional loans are the two most common ways to buy a home. The right one depends mostly on your credit, your down payment and how long you plan to keep the loan.

FHAConventional
Backed byFederal Housing AdministrationFannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines
CreditMore flexibleBetter pricing for strong credit
Mortgage insuranceUpfront and monthly, often for the life of the loan with a small down paymentMonthly, removable once you have enough equity
Property standardsStricter condition requirementsStandard appraisal
CondosProject must be FHA-approvedProject review by the lender
OccupancyPrimary residence onlyPrimary, second home or investment

When FHA fits

  • Your credit score or history is below what conventional pricing rewards.
  • Your debt-to-income ratio is on the high side.
  • You are buying your first home with a small down payment.

When conventional fits

  • You have good credit.
  • You want mortgage insurance that goes away.
  • You are buying a second home or investment property.

Pitfalls to watch for

Comparing payments, not total costLook at how long mortgage insurance lasts on each loan, not just this year's payment.
FHA condition issuesHomes needing repairs can fail FHA property standards. Know the home's condition before choosing FHA.

Questions people ask

Is FHA only for first-time buyers?

No. Anyone who meets FHA guidelines and will live in the home can use FHA. It is popular with first-time buyers because of its credit flexibility.

Which has lower mortgage insurance, FHA or conventional?

It depends on your credit and down payment. FHA mortgage insurance usually lasts longer, often for the life of the loan with a small down payment. Conventional mortgage insurance can be removed once you have enough equity. Compare both on your actual numbers.

Can I switch from FHA to conventional later?

Yes, by refinancing once your credit and equity qualify. Many borrowers do this to remove FHA mortgage insurance.
