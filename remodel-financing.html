Paying for a remodel

Use the equity you already have to remodel your home. How HELOCs and cash-out loans work for a remodel, and the mistakes to avoid.

A remodel is one of the best uses of home equity: you borrow against the house to make the house better. Perra Home Loans does not do construction loans. For most homeowners, a HELOC or a cash-out refinance is the simpler way to pay for a remodel.

Ways to pay for a remodel

HELOC

A line of credit behind your mortgage. Draw money as each contractor invoice comes in and pay interest only on what you have used. Your first mortgage stays the same. This is the most common choice for a remodel. See refinance, cash-out and HELOC for how it works.

Cash-out refinance

Replace your mortgage with a bigger one and take the difference in cash. Makes sense when today's rates are at or below your current rate, or you want one fixed payment.

Buying a home to fix up

Buy with a standard purchase loan, then use a HELOC once you have built equity. Real estate investors who need short-term money to buy and renovate can look at bridge loans from My Bridge Loans.

Pitfalls to watch for

No room in the budgetRemodels almost always find surprises behind the walls. Borrow enough to cover a contingency, or have savings set aside.
Unpermitted workWork done without permits can lower an appraisal, cause insurance problems, and slow a future sale or refinance.
Paying the contractor too far aheadPay as work is completed, not in large amounts up front. A HELOC makes it easy to draw only what each stage needs.
Over-improving for the neighborhoodA house remodeled far beyond the homes around it rarely sells for the full cost of the work.
Borrowing on after-repair valueMost home equity loans are based on today's value. Do not plan a budget that only works if the lender counts the finished value.

Questions people ask

Do you offer construction loans?

No. Perra Home Loans does not do construction or ground-up loans. For a remodel on a home you own, most people use a HELOC or a cash-out refinance.

Is a HELOC good for a remodel?

Often, yes. You draw money as the contractor bills you and pay interest only on what you have drawn, which fits the way remodel costs come in.

Will a remodel increase my home's value?

Some projects add more value than they cost and some do not. Kitchens, bathrooms and added living space usually help. Work done without permits can hurt value and cause problems when you sell or refinance.

Can I borrow against the value after the remodel?

Most HELOCs and cash-out loans are based on the home's current value, not the value after the work is done. Plan the budget around what you can borrow today.
